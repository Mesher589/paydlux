# Boost VC — founder video: script and how to record it

Boost's rules, verbatim from the form: under 5 minutes, **founder(s) only, speaking directly to
camera, no slides, no special effects, no AI-generated content**. If the file is too big, upload a
doc with a YouTube/Vimeo/Loom link instead.

Three things they ask for: (1) why you built this and what you see that others miss, (2) why it
matters when you win, (3) one of three personal questions. This script covers all three in about
**95 seconds**. Read it, do not memorise it — stumbling is fine, they say they want cockroaches,
not polish.

## How to record (iPhone, no setup)

1. Phone **landscape**, back camera, **eye height** — stack it on books, do not shoot up your nose.
2. Face a window. Daylight on your face, window behind the phone. No lamp on your forehead.
3. Room quiet, phone on **Airplane Mode** so nothing interrupts.
4. Sit still, hands visible if you want. Look at the **camera lens**, not at the screen.
5. Open the teleprompter page on the phone: `brand/teleprompter-boost.html` (Files app, tap to
   open in Safari). Start it, read, hit stop.
6. Record 3 takes. Pick the one that sounds most like you talking to a friend.
7. Send me the file and I will trim the head and tail and compress it under the upload limit.
   Plain cuts only — that is allowed; effects and AI are not.

## The script (95 seconds)

> I'm Sebastian Sikora. I'm in Warsaw, I'm retired, and I'm building Paydlux alone.
>
> I got into this by accident. I was bored after retiring, so I started building small projects
> with AI agents, and they kept hitting the same wall. They could do the work — call an API, rent
> compute, buy data — but they had no way to pay for any of it. Nothing existed that I could hand
> an agent and still safely authorise as a human. So I stopped looking for one and built it.
>
> The thing everyone else is missing: everybody is building the agent that buys. Almost nobody is
> building the part that decides whether it's allowed to spend. If the limit lives in the app's
> code, the agent can talk its way around it. So the ceiling is committed into the signed
> transaction itself — on-chain. The agent cannot widen its own authority, and anything outside
> the box stops and waits for a human signature on a phone.
>
> When this wins, money moves between machines at machine speed and a human is still in charge of
> it. Whoever owns that authorisation layer sits under every agent economy that gets built.
>
> It's live on Stellar mainnet now: twenty-three settled transactions, one of them released only
> after a real iPhone signature.
>
> And the personal one — the hobby that has nothing to do with any of this: I built a winter garden
> in my own garden. Foundation, glazing, the whole thing. That's where I got used to building
> something that has to hold up in winter.

Swap the last paragraph if you'd rather answer one of their other two questions (proudest
accomplishment before 21, or the craziest situation you've been in). The winter-garden line is the
one I'd keep: it's true, it's specific, and it explains the temperament without claiming anything.

## What NOT to say

- Don't apologise for being solo, retired, or pre-revenue. If asked about revenue in the call: zero.
- Don't mention the design internals of the vault. The video is for the person, not the architecture.
- Don't add slides, captions, music or cuts to B-roll. They ban it.
